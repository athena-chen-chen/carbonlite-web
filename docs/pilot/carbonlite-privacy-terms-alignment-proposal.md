# CarbonLite Privacy, Terms, and Paid-Pilot Scope Alignment Proposal

**Status:** Review proposal only. No React page has been changed.
**Prepared:** November 5, 2026
**Compared artifacts:** `PrivacyPolicyPage.tsx`, `TermsOfUsePage.tsx`, `PaidPilotScopePage.tsx`, the v1 retention-policy review draft, and the controlled paid-pilot agreement draft.

This proposal identifies customer-facing conflicts and missing disclosures. Suggested language remains subject to business-owner and Alberta/Canadian legal/privacy review. Approved language should be implemented only after the actual operating controls and provider configuration support it.

## 1. Priority definitions

- **P0 before any real-data paid pilot:** A missing or conflicting statement could cause customers to receive a materially inaccurate description of scope, data handling, deletion, providers, calculations, or commercial terms.
- **P1 before agreement signature or account provisioning:** Needed for a coherent controlled-pilot package, but dependent on a P0 decision or final schedule.
- **P2 before broader launch:** Important improvement that need not delay a synthetic-data review if clearly restricted.

## 3. Required implementation sequence

1. Close or explicitly restrict the real-data P0 operational gates.
2. Approve the 15 policy decisions, actual provider register, factor allowlist, quotas, fee, term, support, and recovery wording.
3. Obtain Alberta/Canadian legal/privacy review of the agreement, privacy roles, retention/deletion, cross-border disclosure, consent/acknowledgement, liability, tax, and termination.
4. Verify that each public statement maps to an implemented control and retained evidence.
5. Update the three pages in one reviewed change with tests, new version/effective dates, and change communication. This proposal does not authorize that code change.

## 4. Current conflict summary

- Public scope says 4–5 days and CAD $500–$1,500; the proposal says four weeks and CAD $199.
- Privacy retention is open-ended while the review package proposes class-specific triggers and separate active/backup milestones.
- Terms disclaim recovery but do not explain proposed internal targets, backup survival, or post-restore deletion reconciliation.
- Provider wording lists contemplated services without verified configuration, retention, location, DPA, or deletion evidence.
- The scope acknowledgement addresses output limitations but is not a signed contract, privacy consent, or real-data authorization.
- Prohibited data, export/offboarding, deletion evidence, qualified deletion timing, location-based Scope 2, and “market-based not calculated” need explicit aligned language.
- Contact ownership is inconsistent across Privacy and Terms.
