# CarbonLite RET-002 — 30-Day Active-System Deletion Feasibility

**Assessment date:** November 6, 2026
**Scope:** Founder preference `FDR-2026-002`, contract alignment, local implementation inspection, and manual-operability assessment
**Decision state:** `PENDING_FOUNDER_APPROVAL`
**Evidence level:** `LOCAL_TESTED` for the focused local controls; no provider, deployment, or operational approval evidence
**Customer commitment:** **NO-GO**

## 1. Pending founder preference

`FDR-2026-002` records a preference to complete deletion of covered customer data from active systems within 30 calendar days after the end of the paid pilot, subject to applicable preservation obligations, valid Legal Holds, and expressly disclosed exceptions. The 30 days measure completion, not the start of deletion.

The covered active-system proposal includes original source files, AI/extraction drafts, confirmed Activity Data, calculated emissions, CarbonLite-controlled PDF/CSV report data and artifacts, and the customer organization/account records needed to close the workspace. Audit-record retention and backup/recovery-copy retention remain undecided. Customer-controlled downloads are outside Kach's deletion control.

This is a preference for review. It is not an approved policy, contract term, production procedure, or authorization to process or delete real customer data.

## 3. Contract and policy inconsistencies

1. The agreement states source deletion at pilot end plus 30 days, but states active-system offboarding is 30 days after a later trigger, export window, and clearance. The latter can move completion beyond 30 days after pilot end.
2. The agreement and retention policy leave confirmed records, calculations, AI-job payloads, and report retention contract-defined or pending. `FDR-2026-002` now records a pending preference to include those covered categories in the 30-day active-system completion scope.
3. The retention policy treats moving managed bytes to quarantine as part of active-system deletion, while the bytes remain recoverable on the application-managed volume until a separate purge. Contractual completion must require purge or expressly classify and disclose the retained quarantine exception.
4. The proposed 14-day export opportunity can fit within the 30-day clock, but current language starts or extends the clock after the export window. Under the founder preference, export must occur within the 30 days unless the signed agreement expressly permits a different trigger.
5. The public Privacy Policy uses an open-ended “as long as reasonably needed” standard and broad technical, legal, backup, and operational limitations. It does not state a completion deadline, covered classes, clock, verification, or retained exceptions.
6. The public Terms require customers to keep copies and disclaim permanent storage/recovery, but contain no active-system deletion commitment or separation between active deletion, backup expiry, audit evidence, and customer-controlled exports.
7. The deletion-request runbook measures 30 days from the later of verified request and clearance. The offboarding preference measures from paid-pilot end. The runbook needs a distinct pilot-end case type and must not silently reset the clock for ordinary internal staffing or approval delay.
8. Audit/evidence retention and backup retention remain undecided. A contract cannot accurately disclose their exceptions or maximum survival until those decisions and operational evidence exist.

## 4. Manual 30-day feasibility

For one to three invitation-only organizations, a controlled manual process is operationally plausible in a normal, unheld case but is not yet reliable enough for a customer commitment:

- a weekly review detects a paid-pilot end within at most seven days, leaving at least 23 days;
- a 14-day export window beginning at pilot end leaves 16 days for dry run, approval, erasure, purge, verification, and notice;
- the local operation is bounded and supports dry run, exact confirmation, fail-closed holds/artifacts, retry, and reconciliation; and
- the expected pilot volume is small.

The process is not commitment-ready because no authoritative organization-level pilot-end/deletion-due state, deadline queue, named primary and backup operators, operation-bound independent approval, production deployment evidence, provider artifact coverage, quarantine-purge service target, or backup/evidence disposition exists. A weekly checklist cannot compensate for an unassigned owner, an unsupported artifact, a hold, a failed purge, or missing provider access.

## 5. Minimum viable closure plan

1. **Approve the exact policy boundary.** Founder and qualified legal/privacy authority must approve the covered classes, pilot-end clock, valid exceptions, customer notice, and the meaning of active-system completion. Keep audit and backup decisions separate.
2. **Create one authoritative deadline record.** For each pilot organization, record the signed pilot end, 30-day due date, export deadline inside that clock, hold/exception status, owner, reviewer, execution window, and evidence references. A controlled manual register is acceptable for the first 1–3 organizations if it is reviewed at least weekly and independently checked.
3. **Assign and train roles.** Name a request/offboarding owner, platform-admin operator, different independent reviewer, legal/privacy authority, backup/reconciliation owner, and backups. Do not collapse missing roles.
4. **Bind approval to the exact operation.** Implement the signed second-person control or obtain an explicitly approved, rehearsed compensating control that binds the target, environment, release SHA, inventory, hold result, expiry, and evidence destination.
5. **Close active-storage coverage.** Ensure managed source/report bytes are permanently purged by the deadline, add the missing active-file workspace-erasure E2E assertion, control temporary crash leftovers, and verify Activity Data, calculations, AI jobs, reports, accounts, quotas, feedback, events, and custom factors after erasure.
6. **Close external coverage.** Inventory every provider, external report URL, AI payload, analytics/error record, operator export, and email/support artifact. Add a supported deletion method or exclude/block the store from the pilot.
7. **Decide audit and backup terms.** Approve minimized audit/evidence fields and expiry. Approve and prove backup rotation, maximum survival, hold handling, isolated restore, and deletion replay. Until then, disclose no numeric backup or audit-retention promise.
8. **Deploy and rehearse before commitment.** Freeze the release, verify production configuration without real customer data, run a production-shaped synthetic offboarding rehearsal through erasure, purge, verification, customer notice, backup mapping, failure/retry, and independent review, then record deployment and operational approval.

Automation is not the minimum requirement for the first 1–3 pilots. A tested manual deadline register and staffed workflow can be sufficient. Automatic scheduling becomes necessary if volume, missed reviews, or monitoring evidence makes the manual control unreliable.

## 6. Proposed contract wording

> **Active-system deletion.** Subject to applicable preservation obligations, a valid Legal Hold, and the expressly disclosed exceptions below, Kach will complete deletion of Covered Customer Data from Active Systems no later than 30 calendar days after the Paid Pilot End Date. “Complete deletion” means that deletion has finished and been verified; starting a deletion, disabling access, deleting a database row while managed bytes remain in active or quarantine storage, or scheduling later deletion does not satisfy the deadline. “Covered Customer Data” includes Kach-controlled original source files, extraction and AI drafts, confirmed Activity Data, customer-specific calculated emissions, backend PDF/CSV report data and managed artifacts, and customer organization and account records. It excludes copies downloaded or otherwise controlled by Customer.
>
> **Preservation and Legal Holds.** Kach may suspend deletion only to the extent and for the period required by applicable law, a valid Legal Hold, or another preservation obligation expressly identified in this Agreement. Kach will restrict the affected data, document the exception, and, where legally permitted, tell Customer what category remains, why it remains, and the next review or deletion event. Release of a hold does not itself delete data; Kach will resume the approved deletion workflow and record a revised completion date.
>
> **Audit evidence.** Kach may retain only the minimized deletion, security, and legal evidence expressly listed in Schedule B for the approved period. The content and period are pending and must be completed before signature. Retained evidence is not treated as active customer workspace data, must not contain source content or full activity/emissions payloads, and remains subject to access, minimization, hold, and expiry controls.
>
> **Backups and recovery copies.** Active-system completion does not mean that data has expired from backups or recovery copies. Backup retention, maximum survival, and deletion/replay controls are pending and must be stated in Schedule B before signature. Until expiry, recovery copies are isolated from ordinary customer access and any restore must reapply later approved deletions before access. Kach will not represent backup expiry as complete without verified evidence.

The bracket-free active-system wording is suitable only after the closure plan is evidenced. The audit and backup paragraphs intentionally preserve those decisions as unresolved and therefore prevent signature in the current state.

## 7. GO/NO-GO

- **Manual technical feasibility for a small, normal, unheld case:** **CONDITIONAL GO for continued synthetic rehearsal only.** The local workflow can inventory, guard, quarantine, cascade, retry, purge, and record evidence.
- **Commit to 30-day active-system completion in a customer agreement:** **NO-GO.** The deadline clock, quarantine completion rule, role separation, external/provider coverage, production deployment, and operational rehearsal are incomplete; audit and backup retention remain undecided.
- **Execute deletion or accept real customer data:** **NO-GO.** No approval or production authorization is recorded.
