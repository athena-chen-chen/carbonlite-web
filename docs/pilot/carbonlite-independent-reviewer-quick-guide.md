# CarbonLite Independent Business Reviewer Quick Guide

**Status:** Prepared for a proposed reviewer. The reviewer is not appointed or active until the appointment record is completed and approved.

## What CarbonLite does

CarbonLite helps small organizations organize activity information and prepare draft carbon-emissions records and reports. Customers may upload source documents or enter information. Some information becomes reviewed records; source files, working data, reports, audit evidence, quarantine copies, and backups may have different deletion rules.

You do not need to understand the software framework or database. Your job is to check whether a sensitive business action is clearly requested, properly authorized, fully described, independently reviewed, and supported by evidence.

## Why this role exists

Deletion and Legal Hold release can be difficult to reverse. The Technical Operator must not approve their own destructive action. The Independent Business Reviewer provides a separate human check before execution and reviews the evidence afterward.

This is internal separation of duties. Because the proposed reviewer is related to the founder, it is not automatically external or legally independent. The role is not legal counsel and does not replace a qualified privacy or legal advisor.

## When your review is required

Review is required before:

- a supported customer deletion request is executed;
- a complete organization/workspace erasure is executed;
- Legal Hold release evidence is accepted for the operational step; or
- a previous approval must be renewed because the target, scope, inventory, environment, method, evidence, or expiry changed.

You must also review post-execution evidence when the approved process requires it.

## How to use the checklist

1. Confirm the Request ID and organization reference match every page of the packet.
2. Check that requester identity and authority were verified through an approved method.
3. Confirm the scope is exact. “Delete everything” is not enough.
4. Check the current Legal Hold, retention, contract, and exception results.
5. Compare the dry-run inventory with the proposed action, including active data, quarantine, backups, reports, and unsupported stores.
6. Confirm the named Technical Operator is not you and does not control your approval record.
7. Check the evidence reference/digest and expiry conditions.
8. Select one decision: `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, or `REQUEST MORE INFORMATION`.
9. Give a short reason and record your attributable identity, time, and protected evidence reference.
10. After execution, compare the results with what you approved and record any failure or follow-up.

Approval does not press a button, delete data, release a hold, or permit the operator to bypass safeguards.

## How to decide

**Approve** only when every required item is clear, current, supported, within your role, and free of a recusal conflict.

**Reject** when the request is unauthorized, prohibited, unsafe, outside supported scope, or cannot meet required controls.

**Request more information** when evidence is missing or unclear. This means no execution is authorized.

Never approve because the founder, customer, or operator is in a hurry. Never assume a blank field means “yes.”

## When to ask the founder for more information

Ask the founder or Assigned Owner when the packet lacks an organization reference, exact scope, inventory, operator, customer communication status, backup milestone, evidence reference, or explanation of what will remain.

The founder may supply evidence and business context, but cannot replace a required qualified legal/privacy decision or create your decision for you.

## When to escalate

Stop and escalate to a qualified advisor when the issue involves:

- whether a Legal Hold exists or may be released;
- a legal, privacy, regulatory, contractual, tax, audit, dispute, or preservation requirement;
- uncertain requester authority;
- a conflict of interest or independence requirement;
- customer wording that makes a legal promise; or
- any question outside your documented role or training.

Record only an opaque escalation reference in the operating checklist. Keep the advice itself in restricted storage.

## Confidential records

- Use only your own approved identity; never share an account or signature method.
- Store completed checklists and evidence only in the approved restricted store.
- Do not copy customer files, identity documents, legal analysis, passwords, tokens, or full request messages into spreadsheets, tickets, chat, or this repository.
- Use Request IDs and restricted evidence references instead of personal or customer details.
- Do not change or overwrite historical approvals. Correct a mistake with a new linked record.
- Report suspected disclosure or tampering immediately and stop the review.

## Synthetic example: customer requests workspace deletion

Synthetic customer `ORG-EXAMPLE-001` asks to delete its workspace under request `DR-EXAMPLE-001`.

The packet shows verified requester authority, the exact organization reference, a current Legal Hold result, retention clearance, a dry-run inventory, the Technical Operator, affected quarantine/backups, and an evidence digest. You notice the inventory lists an external report artifact without a supported deletion method.

Choose **REQUEST MORE INFORMATION** and record: “External report artifact is unsupported; complete inventory and supported deletion evidence required.” Do not approve the other items separately and do not ask the operator to delete around the problem. The Assigned Owner must resolve or remove the unsupported target through an approved process and submit a new complete packet.

## Resignation or replacement

You may resign by giving written notice through the approved restricted channel. State the effective time and identify any pending reviews without copying customer details. The owner must assign a replacement, transfer only authorized pending records, revoke your access and active sessions, and preserve historical decisions under your identity.

You must stop reviewing immediately when the appointment expires, is revoked, a conflict requires recusal, access is not secure, or required training/controls are no longer current.
